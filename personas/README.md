# Tournament expert dossiers

These files are evidence-backed **Design DNA / method dossiers**, not impersonation claims. They exist to make each tournament contestant apply a distinct expert's documented frameworks, priorities, critique habits, shipped-work lessons, and stage-specific decision logic to the target app.

The goal is not “use this person's aesthetic.” The goal is: **given the documented body of work, what decisions would this design method strongly push us toward in this stage?**

## Research quality standard

A persona dossier is only tournament-ready when it contains:

1. **Evidence base** — primary sources where available: the person's own site/writing, publisher descriptions of their frameworks/books, official case studies, official award material, or first-party portfolio material.
2. **Core beliefs** — principles extracted from repeated documented work, not generic UX/UI advice.
3. **Stage translation** — explicit instructions for how those principles change Architecture, UX, or UI decisions in DesignLab.
4. **Decision rules** — concrete if/then-style guidance that a model can apply to the target app.
5. **Characteristic critique questions** — the questions this method should repeatedly ask when reviewing its own proposal.
6. **Required output character** — what should make the contestant's result recognizably different from the other three entries.
7. **Failure modes / anti-caricature** — what superficial imitation would look like and what the contestant must avoid.
8. **Whole-route mandate** — the method applies to every reachable user-facing route, not just home/onboarding/hero screens.
9. **Capability discipline** — existing capabilities can be radically restructured/restyled; genuinely new capability remains explicitly proposed for human approval.

## Differentiation contract

The four entries in a stage should **not converge on the same safe redesign**.

### Architecture divergence
- Garrett should win arguments through dependency and conceptual coherence.
- Morville should win arguments through findability, taxonomy and meaningful paths.
- Wroblewski should win arguments through mobile attention, priority and efficient access.
- Cooper should win arguments through user goals, activities and removal of implementation-shaped structure.

### UX divergence
- Cooper asks: “Why is the user doing software-created work?”
- Wroblewski asks: “Why are we spending taps, typing and mobile attention on this?”
- Clark asks: “Why can't the user act directly on the thing, and how does it feel in the hand?”
- Walter asks: “What is the person feeling here, and does the product respond appropriately?”

### UI divergence
- Kuznetsov should be the boldest technologically expressive/spatial concept.
- Meng To should be the strongest design-system-to-production bridge.
- Flarup should be the strongest app-specific identity/personality concept.
- de With should be the strongest premium tactile/native instrument concept, particularly on complex surfaces.

If two contestants are producing substantially interchangeable proposals, the prompt/model should return to the dossiers and strengthen the method-specific choices rather than accepting generic consensus.

## Source-grounding rule

Dossiers may summarize and operationalize published ideas but should not reproduce long copyrighted passages. Source links are retained for traceability and future refreshes. Claims that are uncertain, dated, or primarily inferred from portfolio work should be treated as design evidence rather than falsely attributed quotations.

## Maintenance

Re-research a dossier when:
- the expert publishes major new work that materially changes the method;
- the dossier repeatedly produces generic or convergent tournament entries;
- source links disappear or evidence proves weak;
- DesignLab adds a new stage requiring a different slice of the person's expertise.

The dossiers are reusable across apps. Per-app research belongs in the separate **Current App Truth Pack** and **Capability Research Pack**, keeping recurring token cost low.
